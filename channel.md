# Channel profile: Explained by Simulation (@ExplainedBySimulation)

Every yt-* skill reads this file first. Keep it current: a consistent channel is what YouTube's
monetisation reviewers want to see.

## Niche
Real science, impossible scenarios, visualised. Long-form "what if" videos about space and physics:
take one impossible scenario and follow it step by step using real physics, from the first second
to millions of years later. This is the long-form version of the channel's Shorts.

## Suitable for all ages
Family-friendly in every video: no gore, no swearing, nothing frightening for its own sake. Disasters are
explained through physics, scale and numbers, not suffering. Uploads are **not** marked "Made for Kids" (that
setting turns off comments and cuts revenue), but a 10-year-old and their parents should both enjoy every video.

## Audience
Curious people aged 13-45 who found the channel through a Short. They watch long videos in the
background or before sleep, and like "wait, really?" facts backed by real science. Many aren't
scientists, so everything has to make sense by ear, without equations on screen.

## Narrator
- **Voice: `am_puck`.** Every video uses it, so the channel has one recognisable narrator. The other
  approved voices are `af_heart` and `am_michael`. To switch, change it here, and new scripts will use it.
- **Persona:** a punchy, confident guide who walks the viewer through the scenario as it unfolds: "Ten
  seconds in, you'd notice..." Short, hard-hitting lines on the big moments ("No explosion. No warning.
  Just... gone."), with everyday comparisons ("as heavy as 40 elephants", "faster than a rifle bullet").
  The hype is in the delivery and the edit, never in the facts: clearly separate what physics predicts
  from what's educated guessing.

## Edit style: phonk edit meets science explainer
- **Hype moments** (intensity 0.6+): the music drop lands on the big word, with a beat-synced zoom punch,
  flash, shake and RGB split, a slam-text punchline, and 808 and reverse-swell hits. Aim for one every 2-3 minutes,
  and always in the first 15 seconds (the hook).
- **Breathers** (intensity under 0.35): whiteboard explanations and charts, with calmer music sitting back.
  They make the next drop hit harder, so don't skip them.
- Everything in between (0.35-0.6) gets a light punch on each bar line.
- **Shock drops every 60-90 seconds:** build up for a sentence or two, then the reveal hits on one word with the
  music drop, a blinding flash and a slam-text punchline. Each drop is bigger than the last.
- **Every scene change is a visible transition** (glitch, zoom punch, whip, spin, shutter, flash), each with its own sound.
- **Burned-in captions** on every video: big bold words at the bottom, with the word being spoken highlighted.

## Video lengths
- Long-form: **12-15 minutes** by default (about 1,800-2,250 words of narration at 150 wpm). If a topic
  is genuinely detailed and interesting all the way through, it can run longer (up to about 25 minutes),
  but never pad a video to make it longer
- Two long-form videos a day
- Shorts: handled by the separate PC pipeline. This setup makes long-form only

## Formats to rotate
Rotate through these so the channel doesn't look mass-produced:
1. Timeline what-if: one scenario followed from 1 second to 1 billion years ("What If the Sun Disappeared?")
2. Ranked: counting down to the most extreme ("Every Planet Ranked by How Fast It Would Kill You")
3. Versus: two scenarios or worlds compared, with a clear verdict ("Falling Into Jupiter vs Falling Into the Sun")
4. Real mystery: a genuine open question, investigated with what science currently knows ("Why Is Venus Spinning Backwards?")

Good fits for long versions of existing Shorts: the Sun going boom, jumping on Pluto, the Moon twice as
close, the air vanishing, the Sun disappearing, Earth stopping spinning, Earth collapsing into a black hole.

## Visuals and sound: every video is 100% unique
**Nothing rendered gets reused between videos.** Every animation, effect, sound and drawing is generated
new for that video's script. Reusing code tools is fine (a function that draws a planet); reusing their
output (a rendered clip, a sound file) is not.

- **Animations:** most scenes use one of 73 randomised templates (detailed storyboard set pieces, space simulations, whiteboard diagrams,
  data reveals, cinematic titles), filled in with that scene's facts. The 3-5 hero moments per video get bespoke
  animation code. A template never looks the same twice: layout, colours, camera and effects are seeded per video
- **Whiteboard scenes:** diagrams drawn stroke by stroke on screen, with labels and arrows written in as the narrator explains
- **VFX:** glows, shockwaves, particles, heat shimmer, starfields. Generated procedurally with that video's own seed and settings
- **SFX:** synthesised for each video and timed to its animation events (whooshes, impacts, rumbles, risers, ticks)
- **Music:** the one exception: real tracks from the YouTube Audio Library (free to use on YouTube, no copyright
  claims), Pixabay or NCS. **Instrumentals only**, no explicit lyrics. Tracks are picked to fit each section's energy, and the least-used tracks go first, so the same track
  doesn't come back until the others have had a turn. Each video still gets its own cut: different
  tracks, sections and drop placements, all synced to that video's narration
- **Thumbnail:** rendered from that video's own animation, plus bold text
- **Raw ingredients allowed:** NASA/ESA images and planet textures (used as material inside new animations,
  never as a standalone reused clip), the channel's narrator voice, fonts and colour palette (these are the brand)
- **Uniqueness check:** before upload, frames sampled from the video are compared with every past video. Any match blocks the upload
- Label simulated scenes as simulations or illustrations. Never pass them off as real footage
- Never use the NASA logo or suggest NASA endorses the channel. Credit ESA/Hubble/Webb images (CC BY 4.0) in the description

## Upload schedule
- Long-form: **two a day**, uploaded as private for the user to check and publish (the same as the Shorts process)

## Never
- Invent facts, numbers or sources. Every number needs a source (NASA, ESA, university pages, peer-reviewed papers, textbooks)
- Present speculation as settled science. Use "physicists estimate..." or "one model suggests..."
- Cover pseudoscience, flat earth, astrology or doomsday claims presented as real predictions
- Copy another creator's script, title, thumbnail or footage
- Use music that isn't from the YouTube Audio Library or NCS (always with its credit line in the description), or film or TV clips the channel doesn't own
